# Benchmark results: 1,036 CUTEst problems

Version 0.14.0, 17 October 2026. This is the first run of the harness planned
in [benchmark-harness-plan.md](benchmark-harness-plan.md) (gap 6 in
[gaps.md](gaps.md)). Every number below comes from
[benchmarks/tables.md](benchmarks/tables.md), generated by
`python -m bench.report` from the raw results in
`benchmarks/results.jsonl.gz`. The recommendations are marked as such; the
defaults are your decision, and none was changed.

## Summary

- **No false claim.** The 11 sqp-craft configurations and the experiment
  made no false convergence claim in 12,432 runs. The comparators' claims
  failed the same check 154 times (SLSQP), 80 times (Uno, IPOPT preset), 4
  times (Uno, filterSQP preset with exact Hessians) and once (Uno, filterSQP
  preset).
- **The active-set backend beats the default.** Clarabel solves 754
  problems and the active-set backend 799. The active-set backend solves 54
  that Clarabel does not; 47 of those end in `qp_failure` with Clarabel.
  Clarabel solves 9 that the active-set backend does not. The active-set
  backend is also 2% cheaper in callbacks and 4% in time.
- **Elastic mode is the only solver option that pays on its own.** It adds
  14 problems net to the active-set backend (813) at 0.7% more callbacks.
  Declaring the linear rows, which is input rather than an option, adds 10
  (809).
- **The other options do not pay on CUTEst.** The second-order correction,
  the augmented-Lagrangian merit, the curvature repair and Jacobian scaling
  each solve 2–6 fewer problems than the plain active-set backend, at equal or
  higher cost.
- **sqp-craft is ahead of every quasi-Newton comparator here.**
  - Uno with the filterSQP preset and L-BFGS solves 760; the active-set
    backend solves 84 problems Uno does not, and Uno 45 it does not.
  - On problems both solve, the active-set backend needs 7% fewer callbacks
    and 15% less time.
  - SLSQP solves 631 and Uno with the IPOPT preset 713.
- **Second derivatives are the largest lever.** Uno's filterSQP with exact
  Hessians solves 845 problems against 760 with L-BFGS. It needs 54% fewer
  callbacks, and 70% fewer than sqp-craft on the control problems.
- **The failures point to three cheap fixes and one large item:**
  - **The first step:** on 34 problems sqp-craft fails at iteration 0, where
    the BFGS start B₀ = I gives a step too long for 25 halvings (on 32 of
    them ‖∇f(x₀)‖∞ is between 10⁴ and 10²⁷). A crude emulation of a
    first-step rule recovers 16 problems net.
  - **Nonfinite values:** 20 runs on 6 problems end in an exception instead
    of a status.
  - **Infeasible problems:** 93 of the 131 problems no solver solves are
    probably infeasible from their start. sqp-craft reports
    `restoration_limit` there, or `locally_infeasible` with elastic mode,
    without a certificate.
  - **Exact Hessians** (gap 2) are the large item.

## What was run

**Problems.** S2MPJ at commit `fea6a70` has 1,104 problems. 1,036 pass the
rules of the plan. The 68 excluded are:
- 21 that cannot be set up and evaluated at the start: 18 take over 60 s,
  and 3 fail to load;
- 19 with m > 512 and 11 with n > 256;
- 9 non-smooth problems;
- 8 whose evaluation takes over 0.2 s.

The selection holds 234 unconstrained, 155 bound-constrained, 148 linearly
constrained and 499 nonlinearly constrained problems, 35 of them
control-like. The problems are small at their default sizes: the median has
n = 8, and 127 problems have n ≥ 50. 105 starts lie outside the bounds and
were clipped into them, for every solver alike.

**Solvers.**
- Eleven sqp-craft configurations: the defaults (Clarabel); the active-set
  backend alone and with each option; a combination of the SNOPT-like
  options; and the augmented-Lagrangian merit with Clarabel.
- Four comparators:
  - SciPy SLSQP;
  - Uno 2.9.1 with the filterSQP preset (L-BFGS, trust region, filter, BQPD);
  - Uno with the IPOPT preset (L-BFGS, interior point, MUMPS);
  - Uno's filterSQP preset with exact Hessians from S2MPJ.
- One experiment (below).

Budgets were 500 iterations and 60 s for every solve, and 5,000 callbacks
for sqp-craft. The main run took about 3 hours 20 minutes on two cores of an
Intel Xeon at 2.1 GHz, two solves at a time.

**The check.** A run is *solved* when the independent first-order check
passes at its point:
- primal violation ≤ 10⁻⁶;
- stationarity, complementarity and sign residuals ≤
  10⁻⁶·max(1, ‖∇f‖∞).

The check uses the solver's multipliers, or least-squares multipliers.
sqp-craft's own test is the same absolute 10⁻⁶. The ranking does not hinge
on that: with every threshold of the check 100 times looser, the order of the
solvers is unchanged (active-set 815, Uno filterSQP 788, Uno with exact
Hessians 862; see the sensitivity table).

### Q1: the QP backend

The active-set backend solves 45 more problems net (54 against 9).
- **Why:** 47 of the 54 end with Clarabel in `qp_failure`. Clarabel ends
  120 runs in `qp_failure`, against 24 for the active-set backend. Its interior-point
  QPs fail on degenerate and nearly infeasible subproblems, as on the
  complementarity family (gap 6).
- **Cost:** on the 745 problems both solve, the active-set backend needs 2%
  fewer callbacks (42.9 → 42.0) and 4% less time (0.275 → 0.263 s, shifted
  geometric means). 610 of the 745 take the same number of callbacks.
- **Clarabel's 9:** the active-set backend ends 3 of them in
  `line_search_failure` and 2 in `qp_failure`.

**Recommendation:** make `qp_solver="active-set"` the default.

### Q2: the merit function

- **Second-order correction:** it loses 3 problems and wins none. It is
  more expensive on 111 problems and cheaper on 17. CUTEst's curved
  constraints rarely cause the Maratos effect it targets, and the corrected
  steps cost a function evaluation each.
- **Augmented-Lagrangian merit:**
  - with the active-set backend, 6 more problems and 12 fewer; costs equal;
  - with Clarabel, 9 more and 17 fewer;
  - on 3 problems (BRATU2DT, LUKVLE1, SEMICN2U) its trial-point merit
    overflows and the solve ends in an exception, a bug.

**Recommendation:** keep the ℓ1 merit, with the second-order correction off.
The smooth merit's gains on the stress families
([snopt-lessons.md](snopt-lessons.md)) do not carry over to CUTEst.

### Q3: the curvature repair

`hessian_update="augmented"` solves 1 problem the damped update does not,
and loses 3. It costs 0.7% more callbacks.

**Recommendation:** keep damping.

### Q4: scaling

- **Jacobian scaling:** 8 more problems and 11 fewer, and 6.6% more
  callbacks, the largest cost of any option. CUTEst problems come in units
  their authors chose; the badly scaled orbit copies that Jacobian scaling
  rescued are not represented here.
- **Linear scaling** on top of declared linear rows changes no outcome and
  costs 1.8% more callbacks.

**Recommendation:** keep scaling off, and revisit with the target
application's units.

### Q5: declared linear rows

Declaring S2MPJ's linear rows solves 14 more problems and 4 fewer, at the
same callbacks. It needs the user to know the rows, so it is input, not a
default.
- **The 14:** mostly nonlinear-equation problems with linear rows
  (LEVYMONE*, EGGCRATENE, EXTROSNBNE, BROWNBSNE), where restoration or the
  QP otherwise fails.
- **The 4:**
  - RECIPE's model is undefined at the projected start point, so the solve
    aborts as documented;
  - AVION2 ends in `line_search_failure`, FLETCHER and JANNSON3 in
    `restoration_failed`.

**Recommendation:** document that applications should declare their linear
rows. The harness does so in its `+linear` configurations.

### Q6: elastic mode

Elastic mode solves 22 problems the active-set backend does not (12 of them
end in `restoration_limit` without it) and loses 8. It costs 0.7% more
callbacks. It also changes how failures end:
- Of the active-set backend's 121 restoration failures, elastic mode solves
  10 and ends 52 as `locally_infeasible`.
- In all, 60 unsolved runs end `locally_infeasible`. That verdict is local:
  on 11 of those problems another sqp-craft configuration finds a KKT point,
  and on 4 a comparator does.

**Recommendation:** make `elastic_mode=True` the default with the active-set
backend. Together with Q1, that is 813 solved against 754 today.

### The SNOPT-like combination

It combines the augmented-Lagrangian merit, the curvature repair, declared
linear rows with linear scaling, elastic mode and a violation limit of 10.
- It solves 812 problems, 13 more net than the active-set backend (33
  against 20).
- It reaches the best objective most often of the sqp-craft configurations
  (732).
- It costs 2% more callbacks.

Its gains come from elastic mode and the linear rows. Its losses follow its
parts: elastic mode alone also loses ALLINITA and ALLINITC, and the merit
alone ARTIF, ORTHREGD and ORTHRGDM.

### Q7: where sqp-craft stands

On the same problems, from the same starts, judged by the same check:

| pair | only first | only second | callbacks, both solve | time (s), both solve |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| active-set vs Uno filterSQP (L-BFGS) | 84 | 45 | 38.5 vs 41.4 | 0.218 vs 0.255 |
| active-set vs Uno IPOPT (L-BFGS) | 120 | 34 | 36.6 vs 54.9 | 0.191 vs 0.280 |
| active-set vs SLSQP | 190 | 22 | 35.2 vs 40.4 | 0.199 vs 0.297 |
| clarabel vs Uno filterSQP (L-BFGS) | 69 | 75 | 37.6 vs 40.1 | 0.217 vs 0.244 |

- **The quasi-Newton comparators:** sqp-craft's dense BFGS with an
  active-set QP is more robust than Uno's filterSQP with L-BFGS on these
  small problems, and cheaper. Uno's run ends 139 times at the iteration
  limit, which suggests that L-BFGS curvature is the weaker part there, not
  the filter.
- **Control-like problems (35):**
  - every active-set configuration solves 33, except the one with Jacobian
    scaling (32);
  - Clarabel 32, Uno filterSQP 31 (both Hessian options), Uno IPOPT 29 and
    SLSQP 27;
  - on the 31 that both the active-set backend and Uno filterSQP with L-BFGS
    solve, sqp-craft needs more callbacks (39.9 against 33.2) and less time
    (0.32 against 0.35 s).
- **SLSQP:** 154 of its successes fail the check. It stops on a small change
  in f, not on first-order conditions. It also crashed the process 16 times
  with heap corruption, a SciPy 1.18.1 defect: all 16 are nonlinear-equation
  problems with many more equality rows than variables.
- **Uno with the IPOPT preset:** 80 of its claims fail the check. 50 of
  them pass with thresholds 100 times looser: IPOPT scales its dual test.

### Q8: what second derivatives would buy

Uno's filterSQP preset with exact Hessians, against the same preset with
L-BFGS:
- **Solved:** 845 against 760. Exact Hessians solve 109 problems L-BFGS does
  not, and L-BFGS solves 24 they do not.
- **By subset:** the gain is in the unconstrained (200 against 156) and
  bound-constrained (149 against 120) problems. With nonlinear constraints it
  is small (354 against 352).
- **Cost:** callbacks fall by 54% (42.5 → 19.5) on the problems both solve.
  On the control problems that sqp-craft and the exact-Hessian run both
  solve, the exact run needs 11.6 callbacks against sqp-craft's 38.8.
- **Iteration limits:** of sqp-craft's 27 problems that end at the
  iteration limit, exact Hessians solve 13.

For the trajectory application, where each callback is a propagation, exact
or better second-order information (gap 2) is worth about a factor of three
in callbacks. It is not needed for robustness on constrained problems.

### Q9: what fails, and why

The active-set backend leaves 237 problems unsolved; elastic mode leaves 223.

| class | active-set | elastic | a comparator solves (active-set) | what it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| restoration limit or failure | 119 | – | 20 | mostly infeasible starts: 97 on problems where some solver reports infeasibility |
| `locally_infeasible` | – | 60 | – | elastic mode's verdict on most of the same problems |
| `line_search_failure` | 56 | 55 | 38 | 34 at iteration 0 with huge gradients, the first step |
| `iteration_limit` | 27 | 29 | 15 | slow BFGS convergence; exact Hessians solve 13 |
| `qp_failure` | 24 | 36 | 15 | degenerate or inconsistent QPs |
| `numerical_failure`, stalled, others | 10 | 42 | – | |
| an exception (`error`) | 1 | 1 | 0 | HS25NE: the active-set QP returns a NaN step |

**The problems no solver solves.** 131 problems are solved by none of the 15
configurations. On 93 of them some solver reports a stationary point of the
infeasibility, so they are probably infeasible from their start. Many are
systems of equations without an exact solution: 90 of the 131 have at least
as many rows as variables, and 49 are the `…NE` versions of data-fitting
problems.

**The first step.** sqp-craft starts BFGS from B₀ = I. When ‖∇f(x₀)‖∞ is
10⁴ to 10²⁷, as in the NIST data-fitting problems (CHWIRUT*, GAUSS*, MISRA*,
THURBER, …), the first step is as long as the gradient, and 25 halvings
cannot shorten it enough. That happens on 34 problems: 30 unconstrained and
4 constrained. On 25 of them some comparator succeeds, most often Uno with
exact Hessians (22).

**Exceptions instead of statuses.** 20 runs of the 11 configurations, on 6
problems, end in an error:
- HS25NE: the active-set QP returns a NaN step, in all nine active-set
  configurations;
- the augmented-Lagrangian merit overflows at a trial point (BRATU2DT,
  LUKVLE1, SEMICN2U);
- the complementarity or merit residual overflows (LUKVLE6 and SEMICN2U,
  with the correction or Jacobian scaling);
- RECIPE's model is undefined at the projected start.

None is a false claim, but each should end with a status, as a trial point
where the model is undefined does.

## Experiment: a first step of at most unit length

The harness emulates a first-step rule without changing the solver. It
starts BFGS from max(1, ‖∇f(x₀)‖∞)·I (`active-set+b0-gradient` in
`bench/solvers.py`), so the first step is at most of unit length. Resets use
the same matrix, which is cruder than any rule sqp-craft would adopt.

| | only active-set | only experiment | B wins/losses | callbacks | iterations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| active-set → b0-gradient | 16 | 32 | 221/250 | 44.1 → 45.7 | 18.3 → 21.3 |

- **Solved:** 815 against 799, and the best objective on 737 problems, the
  most of any sqp-craft run.
- **The 34 iteration-0 failures:** it recovers 12. Of the rest, it ends 9 in
  `qp_failure` and 5 `stalled`: tiny steps from a huge B₀.
- **Cost:** it slows the many well-scaled problems whose gradient is
  moderately above 1 (16% more iterations). Of its 16 losses, 6 end at the
  iteration limit.

**Recommendation:** implement a targeted rule in Rust and measure it with
the harness. Candidates are a limit on the first trial step, such as SNOPT's
major step limit, or a rescaling of B₀ after the first step
(Shanno–Phua: B₀ ← (yᵀy/sᵀy)·I). Either should keep the gains without the
cost on well-scaled problems.

## Order of the next work, from these results

1. **Defaults (your decision):** active-set backend with elastic mode, 813
   against 754 solved, at slightly lower cost.
2. **A first-step safeguard:** up to 34 problems, 25 of them solved by a
   comparator. It is cheap, and the harness measures it.
3. **Statuses instead of exceptions** for nonfinite QP steps, merits and
   residuals: 6 problems, and robustness for applications.
4. **Infeasibility:** 93 of 131 unsolved problems are probably infeasible.
   An infeasibility certificate would make `locally_infeasible` checkable,
   and the harness's check could verify it. This is the priority-list item
   "infeasibility (steering plus certificate)".
5. **Second-order information** (gap 2): the largest saving in callbacks, a
   factor of 2–3, and 13 of the 27 iteration limits.
6. **QP failures of the active-set backend:** 24 problems, 15 of them solved
   by a comparator.

The watchdog stays low: the second-order correction, its closest relative,
does not pay here.

## Findings about the harness and the comparators

- **S2MPJ's linear rows.** S2MPJ marks a row linear (`lincons`) when its
  group has no nonlinear elements, even under a nonlinear group function.
  ALSOTAME's row is sin(x − y), for example. The adapter drops such rows,
  checking the group function and the Jacobian at three points; 7 problems
  are affected. sqp-craft itself caught the error: its check that declared
  rows keep a constant Jacobian ended those solves with an error, not with a
  wrong result.
- **The selection is frozen.** One evaluation of the slowest problems varies
  by up to ±50% between catalog runs. The 0.2 s rule therefore admitted
  BAmL1SP and BAmL1SPLS in one run and not in the other. `bench.selection`
  keeps the problems of the first run (`--frozen`) and re-derives only their
  attributes.
- **unopy 0.4.15** passes a null pointer for an empty multiplier vector to
  the Hessian callback of an unconstrained problem. The harness adds one free
  row with a zero Jacobian entry, which leaves the problem unchanged. Uno's
  IPOPT preset crashed twice (ECKERLE4, MUONSINE).
- **SciPy 1.18.1 SLSQP** corrupts the heap on problems with more equality
  rows than variables, 16 crashes in all. The workers contain such crashes;
  each is recorded as `crash`.

## Limits

- **Small problems.** The CUTEst default sizes are small: the median n is 8,
  and only 35 problems have n ≥ 100. The cost of dense linear algebra at
  n = 256, where the orbit transfer lives, is not measured here.
- **Python callbacks.** S2MPJ evaluates in Python. Wall times include
  callback time for every solver alike, so they are comparable between
  solvers, but not with published Fortran CUTEst timings.
- **One start per problem**, the S2MPJ start clipped into the bounds.
- **No Ipopt or SNOPT binaries.** Uno's presets reproduce their methods, not
  their implementations.
- **Comparator claims.** Uno's and SLSQP's claims are judged by sqp-craft's
  check. Their own tests differ, so their rejected claims are not errors of
  those solvers.

## Reproduce

```bash
python -m pip install ".[bench]" unopy        # Uno is optional
python -m bench.s2mpj fetch                     # S2MPJ at the pinned commit
python -m bench.catalog catalog.json            # ~13 min on two cores
python -m bench.selection catalog.json selection.json \
    --frozen docs/benchmarks/selection.json
python -m bench.run selection.json results.jsonl --jobs 2   # ~3.5 h; resumable
python -m bench.run selection.json results.jsonl --solvers active-set+b0-gradient
python -m bench.report selection.json results.jsonl tables.md
```

`bench.report` exits with status 1 when a sqp-craft configuration makes a
false claim. The CI job runs 20 Hock–Schittkowski problems the same way.
